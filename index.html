Guide 01

AI voice generators: what to check before you pay

AI voice tools can turn text into narration, help create rough audio drafts, or produce voice tracks in multiple languages. Quality varies by voice, language and use case, so a short test is more useful than relying on a demo.

Use this five-point checklist

  1. Natural sound: test a paragraph with names, numbers and punctuation similar to your real script.
  2. Language and pronunciation: do not assume every voice sounds equally natural in every language.
  3. Editing: check whether you can fix awkward pauses, emphasis and individual words.
  4. Usage rights: read the plan’s terms for commercial projects, client work and distribution.
  5. Limits and price: check monthly character or credit limits, overage rules and cancellation terms.

Practical test: create the same 100–150-word sample in two tools. Rate pronunciation, naturalness, editing time and total cost. Keep the sample and scores so you can compare again if prices or plans change.

Guide 02

Can AI make a useful presentation from a prompt?

AI presentation makers can help turn an outline into a first draft. They are most useful when you already know the audience and purpose. They are less reliable as a substitute for checking facts, choosing the right story, or understanding the subject.

A workflow that keeps you in control

  1. Write the audience, goal and desired length in one sentence.
  2. Provide your own notes or verified source material rather than asking the tool to invent everything.
  3. Generate a first draft, then check every factual claim and number.
  4. Replace generic images and slides that do not support your message.
  5. Export and test the final presentation on the device you will use.

One tool to evaluate is Gamma. Try a small project first and compare the result with your existing workflow. Features and plan limits can change, so check current details on the official site.

Guide 03

Five repetitive tasks you can test with AI

Start with low-risk tasks where a human can quickly check the result:

  1. Turn rough meeting notes into a tidy summary.
  2. Draft a first version of a frequently asked question response.
  3. Convert a long article into a short social post draft.
  4. Organize a list of ideas into categories or next steps.
  5. Rewrite a message for a different audience or tone.

Do not paste passwords, confidential customer details, private employee information or sensitive company documents into a tool unless you have permission and understand its data handling. Always review output before sending it to a customer or colleague.
